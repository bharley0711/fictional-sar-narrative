# Fictional SAR Narrative — Writing Sample

A complete suspicious activity report (SAR) narrative written from an
**entirely invented scenario**, demonstrating narrative structure and style.

> ⚠️ **This is a work of fiction created for portfolio purposes.** The
> subject, accounts, amounts, institutions, and events are made up. It does
> not describe any real person, customer, investigation, or employer matter.
> Fictional names are used throughout, and any resemblance to real persons
> or cases is coincidental.

- [`red_flag_mapping.md`](red_flag_mapping.md) — each red flag in the

## The scenario in one paragraph

A recently opened account, held by a subject whose stated occupation and
expected activity do not fit the observed transactions, receives structured
cash deposits at multiple branches over five consecutive business days,
followed by a single outbound wire to an unrelated third party. The subject
declined to explain the source of the funds when asked. The institution
filed on structuring and unexplainable third-party activity.

## Writing principles applied

Following FinCEN's published SAR narrative guidance, the narrative:

- Answers **who, what, when, where, why, and how** in the first two
  paragraphs.
- Is **chronological**, with exact dates, amounts, and account identifiers
  (fictional) carried consistently throughout.
- Describes the activity **factually**, without legal conclusions — it
  explains why the activity is suspicious rather than declaring a crime.
- Covers the **full review period** and the total amount involved.
- Introduces every subject and account before referring to them, and spells
  out abbreviations on first use.
