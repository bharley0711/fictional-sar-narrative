# Red Flag Mapping — Fictional SAR Scenario

Each red flag in the fictional scenario, and the public source or typology
it reflects. All references are to published material — FinCEN guidance and
advisories, the FFIEC BSA/AML Examination Manual, and the BSA statute — of
the kind an investigator would cite when documenting a real case.

| # | Red flag in the scenario | Public basis |
|---|--------------------------|--------------|
| 1 | Five cash deposits of $9,200–$9,900, each just under $10,000, over five consecutive days | Structuring to evade the CTR requirement — 31 USC 5324; FFIEC BSA/AML Manual, "Currency Transaction Reporting" and SAR guidance on structured transactions |
| 2 | Activity began three days after account opening | New-account risk: activity inconsistent with the purpose stated at onboarding — FFIEC Manual, customer due diligence expectations |
| 3 | Deposits split across three different branches | Use of multiple locations to conduct similar transactions, a recognised structuring indicator — FinCEN structuring guidance and advisories |
| 4 | $47,600 in five days vs. stated expected activity of ~$3,000/month in checks | Transactions inconsistent with the customer's stated occupation, profile, and expected activity — FFIEC Manual, "Suspicious Activity Reporting" red flags |
| 5 | Cash-intensive activity where the customer stated income arrives by check | Activity inconsistent with the stated source and form of income — FFIEC Manual red flags |
| 6 | Subject declined to explain the source of funds; said he "did not want to deal with paperwork" and ended the call | Customer evasiveness / refusal to provide supporting information when asked — FFIEC Manual red flags; the statement itself supports the inference of intent to evade |
| 7 | No documentation of a legitimate source for the funds | Absence of an apparent lawful or business purpose — SAR activity type "no apparent lawful purpose," FinCEN SAR instructions |

## A note on intent

For structuring, the pattern alone is not the whole case: 31 USC 5324 turns
on the transactions being conducted *for the purpose of evading* the
reporting requirement. That is why the narrative treats the customer's own
statement ("did not want to deal with paperwork") and the multi-branch
pattern as important facts, and states them plainly rather than arguing a
legal conclusion.
