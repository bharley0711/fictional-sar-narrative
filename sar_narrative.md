# SAR Narrative — FICTIONAL WRITING SAMPLE

> **Disclaimer:** This narrative is a work of fiction written to demonstrate
> SAR writing. "Harborview Bank," "Daniel A. Reyes," and every account
> number, date, and amount below are invented. It does not describe any
> real person, institution, or investigation.

---

**Subject:** Daniel A. Reyes (DOB 03/14/1988), account ending 4417
**Activity period:** January 12, 2026 – January 21, 2026
**Total amount involved:** $47,600.00
**Filing type:** Structuring (31 CFR 1020.320 / 31 USC 5324); transactions
with no apparent lawful purpose

Harborview Bank ("the Bank") is filing this Suspicious Activity Report on
Daniel A. Reyes ("Reyes"), holder of personal checking account ending 4417
("the account"), for structured cash deposits totalling $47,600.00 conducted
between January 15, 2026 and January 19, 2026. The Bank determined the
activity was suspicious because the deposits were conducted in amounts
consistently just below the $10,000 currency transaction reporting
threshold, were inconsistent with Reyes's stated occupation and expected
account activity, and Reyes declined to provide an explanation for the
source of the funds when contacted by the Bank.

Reyes opened the account on January 12, 2026 at the Bank's Fairfax branch.
At account opening, Reyes stated his occupation as a self-employed
landscaper with expected monthly account activity of approximately $3,000 in
deposits, consisting primarily of customer payments by check. Reyes provided
a Virginia driver's license and a Social Security number that the Bank
verified through its standard customer identification program. No adverse
information was identified at account opening.

Between January 15, 2026 and January 19, 2026, Reyes conducted five cash
deposits into the account at three different Bank branches, as follows:
$9,800.00 on January 15, 2026 (Fairfax branch); $9,500.00 on January 16, 2026
(Herndon branch); $9,900.00 on January 17, 2026 (Fairfax branch); $9,200.00
on January 18, 2026 (Reston branch); and $9,200.00 on January 19, 2026
(Herndon branch). The five deposits totalled $47,600.00. Each deposit was
conducted in cash, each was in an amount between $9,200.00 and $9,900.00,
and none exceeded the $10,000 reporting threshold. No other cash deposits
appear in the account during the review period, and the pattern began three
days after the account was opened. The Bank notes that the use of multiple
branches for same-week cash deposits of similar amounts is consistent with
an effort to avoid attracting attention to the activity.

Prior to these deposits, the account's only activity was the $100.00 opening
deposit on January 12, 2026. The cash deposit activity is inconsistent with
Reyes's stated occupation and expected activity: Reyes indicated deposits
would consist of customer checks totalling approximately $3,000 per month,
while the account instead received $47,600.00 in cash over five days.

On January 21, 2026, a Bank investigator contacted Reyes by telephone to
inquire about the source of the cash. Reyes stated that the funds came from
"a side job" but declined to identify the payer, the nature of the work, or
why the work was paid in cash in the amounts deposited. When asked why the
deposits were each below $10,000, Reyes stated he "did not want to deal with
paperwork" and ended the call. The Bank was unable to obtain documentation
supporting a legitimate source for the funds.

Based on the foregoing, the Bank concluded that Reyes conducted the five
cash deposits in a pattern consistent with structuring transactions to evade
the currency transaction reporting requirement, and that the activity had no
apparent lawful purpose consistent with the customer's profile. The Bank has
restricted the account pending exit review. Supporting documentation,
including deposit slips, branch surveillance references, and the call log
for the January 21, 2026 customer contact, has been retained in accordance
with 31 CFR 1020.320(d) and is available upon request. The Bank has not
notified the subject of this filing.
